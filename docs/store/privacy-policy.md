# Antiphon privacy policy

_Last updated: October 14, 2026_

Antiphon keeps playlists in step between Spotify and Apple Music. This policy explains what happens to your data when you use it.

## The short version

Antiphon doesn't collect any data. There's no Antiphon server and no Antiphon account. The app talks to Spotify and Apple Music directly from your device.

## What stays on your device

- **Your sign-ins.** The Spotify sign-in (and the Spotify client ID you create) is stored in the iOS Keychain. Apple Music access is granted through iOS and managed by Apple.
- **Your seams and history.** Which playlists are linked, the matched tracks, your choices in Review and the sync history are stored in Antiphon's storage on your device. If you use the widgets, a summary is shared with them through an App Group on the same device.

Deleting the app, or using Settings › Reset all data, removes this data.

## Who Antiphon talks to

- **Spotify**, to read your playlists and add or remove tracks in the playlists you link. This uses Spotify's Web API with the client ID you create; Spotify's privacy policy applies to your Spotify account: https://www.spotify.com/legal/privacy-policy/
- **Apple Music**, through Apple's MusicKit, to read your library playlists, search the catalog and add tracks. Apple's privacy policy applies: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

Antiphon only reads and edits playlists. It never plays, follows, likes or posts anything on your behalf.

## What Antiphon doesn't do

- No analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no crash-reporting service.
- No data is sold or shared with anyone.
- Nothing is sent to the developer.

## Notifications

If you allow notifications, Antiphon shows local notifications (for example, when a sync needs your decision). They're created on your device; no push service is involved.

## Children

Antiphon isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly process any child's data.
